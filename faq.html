Ask away.

Does Glassy come with music?

No. It plays the music and video files already on your computer. It isn't a streaming service.

What does it cost?

Nothing. There's no subscription.

Do I need an account?

No.

What's with the glass?

The panels really do bend what's behind them near their edges, like a thick slab of glass. You can tint them any colour, and put moving colours, the song's cover or your own picture behind them.

What files does it play?

Music: mp3, m4a, aac, flac, wav, ogg and opus. Video: mp4, m4v, webm, mkv and mov.

Does it upload my music?

No. Your files stay on your computer. When Glassy looks something up, it sends the song or artist name. Privacy has the full list.

Does it change my files?

Only when you ask. Editing a song's details or cover saves them into the file if it's an MP3. Otherwise your files stay as they are.

Where do the lyrics come from?

A .lrc or .txt file next to the song, the song's own tags, or online. If it finds the wrong ones, search as you type and pick the right match.

Does it work without internet?

Yes. Your library, playback and sound settings all work offline. You only need internet to look up lyrics, artist info and covers, and to check for updates.

Can I try it without installing?

On Windows, yes. The portable version is one file you just open. Get it here.

Why does my Mac say it can't check the app?

Glassy isn't signed with an Apple developer account yet. The Download page shows how to open it, and it takes about a minute.

How do updates work?

On Windows the installed version updates itself and asks you to restart. The portable version and the Mac version tell you when a new one is out. You can check by hand in Settings.

Is there a phone version?

An iPhone app is being worked on. There's no date yet. See the roadmap.

Is Glassy open source?

No. You're welcome to download and use it, but the code, design and name aren't free to copy. The releases and notes are on GitHub.
